Aotani Apartment: Conditions for Rent

Please read these conditions carefully before applying to rent Aotani Apartment.

Furniture, Appliances & Household Items

There is currently a variety of furniture and appliances in the apartment. We generally leave any furniture (sofas, beds, dressers, etc.) or appliances (toasters, microwaves, air conditioners, fridges, washing machines, etc.) that vacating tenants do not take with them for future tenants. We will ensure there is enough furniture for a tenant to be able to move in at any time. However:

  1. We make no guarantees about what kind of furniture there is, or the state of the furniture.
  2. If there are any items in the apartment that you don't want, or if appliances like a fridge or air conditioner break down, we will remove the item at our cost. However, we will not repair or replace any furniture or appliances.
  3. If you want an item removed, please try to tell us within about a month of moving in.
  4. If you buy something while you are at Aotani and leave it after vacating, we will consider it abandoned property.

If you are applying from outside Japan, we can give you a summary of what furniture and appliances are currently in the apartment and/or show you via a video tour (LINE, WhatsApp, Zoom, or Google Meet). If you are renting the apartment, we will not remove any items unless requested to do so.

Kitchen

The kitchen has an IH stove. Water is heated by a boiler outside. We will maintain the stove, boiler, and all piping/equipment needed to provide water and utilities. There are usually assorted pots, pans, and cutlery; however, we do not take responsibility for any of these. If the fridge is operational we will leave it in place, but we do not take responsibility for its maintenance and will not repair or replace it.

Shower & Bathroom

There is a shower and a bath. Water is heated by a boiler outside. We will provide and maintain all associated structures.

Toilet

The toilet is a little different from standard urban toilets. It is what is called kan'i suisen, which is common in rural parts of Japan where there is generally a reduced capacity for sewage disposal. It is a Western-style (toilet seat) flush type (not a pit toilet), working on a reduced flush and pan system rather than a full bowl of water. Waste disposal is done by kumi-tori, meaning sewage goes into a tank under the house and is collected by a truck once or twice a month. These fees are part of the tenant's utilities (currently about ¥1,000/month + ¥220/person, set by Kashiwara City).

Bedrooms & Living Areas

We will ensure that there is basically enough furniture and bedding for a tenant to move in at any time. However, we do not make any guarantees about the amount or state of furniture. If extra bedding, dressers, or other furnishings are required, they are the responsibility of the tenant.

Winter Living in a Traditional Japanese Home

Although Japan can get quite cold, traditional Japanese houses are unfortunately poorly insulated for winter. For tenants who are not used to Japanese winters, please note the following about Aotani during the coldest months (late December to the end of February):

  • Heat individual rooms: It is difficult and expensive to heat the whole apartment at the same time. Most people in Japan choose 2 or 3 main rooms (such as the bedroom and living room) and spend most of their time in those rooms.
  • Kerosene heaters: A common way to heat rooms in Japan and generally cheaper to run than air conditioners. Modern units are advanced, safe to use, and have virtually no odor once running. Air conditioners may also be installed at the tenant's own expense.
  • Warm indoor clothing: Wearing sweaters and thermal underwear ("long johns") indoors is common in Japan and cuts heating costs significantly.
  • Bedding & electric blankets: Feather down bedding is recommended. Electric blankets are relatively cheap to run on low or to pre-heat your bed before getting in.
  • Kotatsu: A low heated table covered with a blanket/quilt that warms your lower body—great for meals or relaxing, widely available, and relatively inexpensive.

Some of the items above we may be able to lend tenants depending on what we have available at the time. At Aotani Apartment there is an air conditioner in the living room and main bedroom, both currently functioning. If an air conditioner breaks down we will remove it at no cost, but we will not maintain or replace air conditioners. If the tenant wishes to replace an air conditioner or install additional units, the costs are borne by the tenant.

Current State of the Apartment

The apartment building is old and parts are worn; however, the apartment itself is basically in good condition and many parts have been recently renovated. There is no major structural damage (such as holes in the wall or fire damage) and nothing is broken. After viewing the apartment, if you have further comments, please attach them as an addendum to the lease. If applying from overseas without viewing in person before moving in, please note any additions within two days of moving in.

Terms of the Lease

  1. The apartment is for residential purposes only. The tenant must maintain the premises in a similar condition to how they were found. Any damage done to the apartment during the tenant's stay will be repaired at the tenant's expense. Please review the "Current State of the Apartment" section above.
  2. We will maintain structural items, wiring, plumbing, etc. Utilities, services, minor/non-structural repairs (such as changing light bulbs, curtains, etc.), and general cleaning are the responsibility of the tenant, including associated costs.
  3. The tenant is responsible for their own insurance. We shall not be held liable for any losses sustained by the tenant while at Aotani. We recommend taking out renter's insurance (ordinary travel insurance usually does not properly cover rented premises). If you cannot arrange renter's insurance from your home country or need help arranging it in Japan, we can help arrange a policy through Tokio Marine Nichido. We can get a quote before you arrive, though the paperwork can only be completed once you are in Japan.
  4. The tenant agrees to follow Japanese municipal procedures (an explanation in English will be provided) for garbage sorting and disposal.
  5. No major alterations to the apartment—including changing or adding locks, screwing things into walls, painting, or wallpapering—shall be made.
  6. Pets allowed with conditions (advance permission required): We don't mind if you have a pet inside the apartment; however, the cost of any damage done by the pet to the property will be borne by the tenant.
  7. Unlike modern apartments, older Japanese buildings require routine maintenance—in particular checking for termites ("white ants") about once every two to three years (mainly under the house) and checking for water leaks. We will need occasional access to the premises for maintenance and pest control, arranged in consultation with the tenant to minimize inconvenience. We may enter without advance notice in an emergency or if the apartment appears abandoned (e.g., tenants no longer answer their phone).
  8. Aotani is in a quiet, peaceful rural area of Japan. The tenant agrees to keep noise to a minimum and make every effort to maintain good relations with the local community. Disturbance caused by loud noise or behavior causing problems in the local community will be grounds for termination of the agreement.
  9. Termination of the agreement:
    • Fixed-term lease: At the conclusion of a fixed-term lease, a new term is possible only through negotiation and mutual consent.
    • Month-to-month rental: May be terminated for any reason by either the landlord or the tenant with 30 days' advance written notice. If a tenant vacates on a date other than the last day of the month, the tenant pays either that month's full rent or ¥3,000 per day, whichever is cheaper.
    • Non-payment of rent: The tenant will be required to vacate within 14 days and outstanding rent will be deducted from the deposit.
    • Unreasonable or unlawful conduct: In the case of conduct considered unreasonable for a tenant or unacceptable under Japanese law, the tenant will be required to vacate within 14 days and remaining rent/deposit will be refunded accordingly. In such cases, locks may be changed without prior consultation.
  10. Final month & move-out procedure:
    • As early as possible in the final month, the tenant will allow us to inspect the premises for any major repairs needed and allow access to complete them before move-out.
    • The tenant agrees to allow access to show prospective future tenants the premises, arranged in consultation with the tenant to minimize inconvenience.
    • A final move-out date will be set for a second final inspection, key collection, and deposit return based on the final state of the apartment.
    • Settlement of bills: Usually we arrange for utilities to be transferred out of the tenant's name on the final day so final bills can be calculated and paid in cash that same day. If you prefer another settlement method, please let us know in advance; the deposit cannot be refunded until all final bills are settled.
    • Deposit return checklist: As long as there are no major issues and all final bills are paid, no part of the deposit will be withheld. Please confirm: kitchen, bathroom, and toilet washed; all rooms cleaned and vacuumed; all garbage removed; and all subscriptions (internet, etc.) cancelled. We prefer to return the deposit in cash on the final day (let us know in advance if you prefer another method).
  11. Upon vacating, the tenant is responsible for removing all personal property. Any personal items left behind will be held for 1 week and then considered abandoned property, which we may use, sell, or dispose of without liability and without further notice.
